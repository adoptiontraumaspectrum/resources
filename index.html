Adoption-Competent (TAC / NTI-Trained) Clinician Directory *

Training Center for Adoption Support and Education (C.A.S.E.)
📍 U.S.-wide · searchable by state

Find therapists who have completed C.A.S.E. adoption-competency training — the 72-hour Training for Adoption Competency (TAC) and/or the web-based National Adoption Competency Mental Health Training Initiative (NTI). Useful where an adoptee-identifying clinician isn’t available locally.* Completion denotes a clinician’s own training and stated competency in adoption work — not measured outcomes for the adoptees they treat. Inclusion is not an endorsement of any individual therapist.

Policy, legal & program record

  • NABSW (1972) — National Association of Black Social Workers position statement opposing transracial adoption of Black children; called for preserving Black families, kinship networks, and identity.
  • CAPTA (1974) — Child Abuse Prevention & Treatment Act. Federal definitions, mandatory reporting, CPS funding; critiqued for a vague “neglect” standard that proxies poverty.
  • ICWA (1978) — Indian Child Welfare Act. Tribal authority over Native child custody; critiqued for having no enforcement body.
  • MEPA (1994) + IEPA (1996) — Multiethnic Placement Act & Interethnic Adoption Provisions; made placement effectively race-blind.
  • ASFA (1997) — Adoption & Safe Families Act. Adoption bonuses and the “15 of 22 months” termination clock.
  • FFPSA (2018) — Family First Prevention Services Act. First turn of Title IV-E money toward prevention.
  • Minnesota African American Family Preservation & Child Welfare Disproportionality Act — SF 716 (2024), Minn. Stat. §§ 260.61–260.693. Prevention-through-policy; rolling out in Hennepin & Ramsey Counties.
  • Louisiana OBC access — Act No. 470 of 2022 (HB 450), amending La. R.S. 40:73. Adults 24+ may obtain their own original birth certificate.
  • EmPwR (Empower Parenting with Resources) — Illinois DCFS + Brightpoint; the largest child-welfare guaranteed-income pilot in the U.S.
  • Positions in the field: Prevention (Toland, J. Kim, Samuels, Baden, McKee) · Reform (NCCPR, RISE) · Abolition (the upEND movement).

How to use this directory & an important note on limitations

“Adoptee-identifying” means the clinician has publicly stated they are an adopted person and works with the adoption constellation. Inclusion is not an endorsement or a guarantee of licensure, availability, or fit — always verify current credentials and ask about adoption-competent, lifespan-oriented care. Where a state shows a gap, that reflects the real, uneven supply of adoptee-centered care (rural, low-income, and non-White adoptees are most affected) — telehealth from a neighboring state or a national directory is often the nearest option.

On adoption-competency training (TAC / NTI): completion denotes a clinician’s own preparation and stated competency in adoption work — not measured outcomes for the adoptees they treat, and not an endorsement of any individual therapist. Treat it as a starting point for vetting, not a guarantee of fit or quality.
